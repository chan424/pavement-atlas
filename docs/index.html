STEP 1

Survey a road image

  1. Open Survey desk from the menu.
  2. Tap Open road image to pick a photo, or Take photo on a phone. You can also tap one of the Reference frames to practise.
  3. Tap Run detector. The first time, the model downloads (about 22 MB) and is then kept on the device.
  4. Coloured boxes appear on the photo. Each box has a damage code and a confidence score (for example D40 82%).
  5. Move the Confidence cutoff slider. Higher hides weak guesses. Lower shows more possible damage.
  6. Tap anywhere on the photo to see that spot magnified in the Detail lens.
STEP 2

Use the live camera

  1. Open Live camera and tap Start camera. Allow camera access when the browser asks.
  2. On a phone the rear camera is used. Tap Switch camera to change.
  3. Hold the phone steady, pointing at the road ahead, about 1–3 metres away. Boxes are drawn as damage is found.
  4. The FPS number shows how many frames per second are checked. ms is the time per check. Phones usually manage 2–6 FPS.
  5. Tap Snapshot & save to keep the current frame with its boxes in the register.
  6. Turn on Auto-log damage with GPS to save geotagged frames automatically while you walk or ride. It saves only when damage is seen and you have moved on (default: every 5 seconds and 20 metres).
  7. To check a recorded video, tap Analyse a video. The video is checked moment by moment and a coloured timeline shows where damage was found. Tap the timeline to jump there.

Camera access works only on a secure (https) address, such as the GitHub Pages site. If the camera is blocked, open the browser's site settings and allow Camera.

STEP 3

Review and edit boxes

  1. Under Box review, every box is listed with its class and score.
  2. Tap Edit to change the damage class, or mark it Accepted (real damage) or Rejected (false alarm). Rejected boxes are hidden but kept for the record.
  3. If the detector missed something, tap ＋ Draw box and drag across the damage on the photo, then choose its class.
  4. Use the Reference / AI detections switch to compare dataset labels or your manual boxes with the detector's boxes.
STEP 4

Save records

  1. Type the Road / route name, for example Ongole – Pernamitta.
  2. Tap Use device location if you are standing at the spot, or type the latitude and longitude.
  3. Add any Field notes (landmark, size, follow-up).
  4. Tap Save survey record. All saved records appear in Field register and on the Dashboard.
  5. To change a saved record, open it from the register, edit and save again.
STEP 5

Use the map

  1. Open Map. Every record with a location is a dot on the map.
  2. Dot colour shows the worst damage on that frame: pothole, alligator crack, transverse crack, longitudinal crack, no damage.
  3. Tap a dot to see the photo, the road name and the damage. Tap Open to review it.
  4. The Condition summary ranks roads from Very poor to Good.
  5. The map needs internet for the background streets. Your records still work offline.
STEP 6

Export, backup and restore

  • CSV: one row per box. Opens in Excel or Google Sheets.
  • GeoJSON: located records for QGIS or other GIS tools.
  • JSON: all records and review details, without photos.
  • Backup: a JSON file with photos. Keep it safe. Use it to move records to another phone or computer.
  • Import: choose a Backup or JSON file to restore. Records with the same ID keep the newest version.

Records are stored only in this browser. Clearing browser data deletes them. Make a Backup regularly.

APP

Install and work offline

  1. On Android Chrome, tap Install app on the Dashboard, or the browser menu → Add to Home screen. On iPhone Safari, tap Share → Add to Home Screen.
  2. Tap Make available offline on the Dashboard once while online. The app and the model are saved on the device.
  3. After that, surveys, the live camera and the register work without internet. Map streets need internet.
READ THIS

Limitations

  • The detector is a small public YOLO11n model trained on Indian RDD2022 images. On its test set it scored 0.402 mAP50: at the default cutoff it found about 4 in 10 labelled defects, and about 1 in 3 of its boxes were false alarms.
  • Transverse cracks (D10) are rare in the training data. At the default cutoff the model found none of the 8 test examples. Check for them yourself.
  • No box does not mean the road is fine. A box does not mean the damage is serious.
  • The app records visible surface damage only. Depth, structural strength, length and repair urgency need a site inspection.
  • The condition bands are a simple guide, not a Pavement Condition Index.
  • Light, shadows, wet roads, motion blur and camera angle change results. Live mode checks a few frames per second, so fast driving may miss damage.
  • Records stay in this browser on this device until you export them.
